Coral reefs are bleaching faster than expected

Marine biologists reported this week that the fourth global coral bleaching event has now affected more than 80 percent of the world's reef area, the largest share ever recorded.

Bleaching happens when water stays too warm for too long and corals expel the algae that feed them. Reefs can recover if temperatures drop, but repeated heat waves leave less time to heal.

Researchers say cutting emissions is the only long-term fix, while short-term efforts focus on breeding heat-tolerant corals and protecting reefs from pollution and overfishing.
