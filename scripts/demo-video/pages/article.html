Octopus

An octopus is a soft-bodied, eight-limbed mollusc. There are about 300 species, found in every ocean.

Contents: Anatomy · Intelligence · Habitat · Life cycle

Anatomy

An octopus has no bones, so it can squeeze through any gap larger than its beak. Each arm is lined with suckers that can taste and feel what they touch, and two thirds of its neurons are in its arms rather than its head.

It has three hearts and blue blood. Two hearts pump blood through the gills, and the third pumps it through the rest of the body.

Intelligence

Octopuses solve puzzles, open jars, and recognize individual people. In aquariums they have been seen escaping their tanks at night and returning before morning.

Habitat

Octopuses live in every ocean, from warm coral reefs to the cold deep sea. Most make dens in rocky crevices, and some carry coconut shells to use as shelters.

Life cycle

Most octopuses live only one to two years. A mother guards her eggs for weeks or months without eating, and dies soon after they hatch.
