Set up Navi

Navigate Chrome with your voice. This takes about a minute.

Allow the microphone

Chrome only lets extensions ask for the microphone from a normal tab like this one, so Navi asks once here.

Choose Navi Free or Pro

Built-in commands (scrolling, tabs, opening sites, “click 5”, the grid) always work, in both.

Free runs on this computer, no cost

Chat, questions about the page, and your own voice commands that chain built-in ones, with Chrome's built-in Gemini Nano. It can't click or type on pages.

Pro your API key

Everything in Free, and it clicks, types and does multi-step tasks for you. You pay the provider directly, usually a few cents an hour.

Get an OpenAI key. It's stored only in this browser profile and sent only to OpenAI.

Download on-device speech

Chrome recognizes your voice on this computer, so audio never leaves your laptop, even while Navi listens for its name. Navi never uses Google's cloud speech service.

Navi's voice also on this computer, free

Kokoro, a natural neural voice: on the graphics chip when there is one (326 MB, once), otherwise on the processor (92 MB). Until it's downloaded, your computer's system voice answers.

Say “Hey Navi”

Navi listens in the background, so you don't need to open anything. It stays awake while you keep giving commands and goes back to sleep after two quiet minutes, or when you say “go to sleep”.

A small caption at the bottom of the page shows what it hears and says. Two shortcuts help with the panel closed:

Press Alt Shift M to talk without saying its name. Type navi and a space in the address bar to type to it.

To see the full activity, click the Navi icon in the toolbar (pin it from the puzzle-piece menu) or press Alt Shift N.

New to Navi? A two-minute spoken tour walks you through it by voice, no screen needed. Start it here, or say “Hey Navi, start the tutorial” any time.

Everything else (voice, listening, privacy, your own commands) is in settings.
