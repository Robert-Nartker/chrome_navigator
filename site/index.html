NAVI · VOICE CONTROL FOR CHROME

Say it.
Navi does it.

Navi is a Chrome extension you run with your voice. Open sites, scroll, click, fill in forms, hear any page read aloud, or hand it a whole task. It hears you on your own computer.

HOW IT WORKS

Instant first.
Smart when needed.

Your voice is turned into words on your own computer. Navi runs the commands it knows straight away, and only sends the rest to an AI.

  1. 01

    You talk

    Navi listens while it's awake and falls asleep after two quiet minutes. “Wake up” or “Hey Navi” brings it back. Off means the mic is closed.

  2. 02

    It hears you, locally

    NVIDIA's Parakeet or Chrome's on-device recognizer write down what you said. Audio never leaves your computer.

  3. 03

    Known commands run

    Dozens of built-in commands, and the common ways they get misheard, match in about 50 ms with no AI.

  4. 04

    The rest goes to the AI

    A quick check sorts real requests from background noise. Real ones go to the AI, which reads the page's buttons and links and acts on them.

INSTALL

Set up in
two minutes.

Navi isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet, so you add it to Chrome yourself. It's a few clicks, once. After setup, nothing needs a click again.

  1. 1

    Download Navi and unzip it

    On Windows, right-click the file and choose Extract All. On a Mac, double-click it. You'll get a folder called navi. Keep it somewhere it won't be deleted, like your Documents folder.

    Allow the microphone

    A setup tab opens by itself. Click Allow microphone, then Allow in Chrome's prompt. This is the one click Chrome requires: extensions can only ask for the mic from a normal tab.

    The same page offers Chrome's on-device speech model. Download it once so your voice is turned into words on your computer.

  6. 6

    Choose Free or Pro

    Free runs a small AI on your computer, Chrome's built-in Gemini Nano. Pro uses your own OpenAI or Anthropic key and can click, type and finish multi-step tasks. Built-in commands work either way, even with no AI at all.

  7. 7

    Open Navi and start talking

    Pin Navi from Chrome's puzzle-piece menu. Press Alt + Shift + N to open its panel, press the power button, and say “what can I say?”

    From then on, Ctrl + Space is push to talk for a single command, whatever state Navi is in. In Settings → Listening you can have Navi start whenever Chrome opens, so it never needs a click.

Updating to a new version

Download the new zip, unzip it, and replace your old navi folder with the new one. Then click the ↻ reload arrow on Navi's card in chrome://extensions. Your settings, API key and your own commands are kept.

Building from source instead

You need Node.js 20 or newer and git.

git clone https://github.com/Robert-Nartker/chrome_navigator
cd chrome_navigator
npm install
npm run build      # the extension is in dist/

Then load the dist folder with Load unpacked as in step 4. npm run dev keeps it up to date with the newest changes on its own.

PRIVACY

Your voice stays
on your computer.

A microphone that's always listening has to earn your trust. Here's exactly what Navi does with what it hears and sees.

  • 🎙

    Speech is recognized locally

    Parakeet or Chrome's on-device recognizer. Navi never uses Chrome's cloud speech service. Navi's voice runs on your computer too.

  • ⏻

    Off means off

    Navi starts off each time Chrome opens: no mic, nothing loaded. Asleep, nothing you say is acted on, kept or sent anywhere until “wake up”.

  • 🏷

    The AI sees labels, not content

    By default it gets the names of buttons, links and fields. It reads page text only when your request needs it. Password fields are never included.

  • 🛡

    Pages can't give it orders

    Text on a page is marked as untrusted, and the AI is told never to follow instructions found in it.

  • 🚫

    Sites you choose stay off limits

    List your bank or health portal, and the AI is never used there. Reading a page aloud never involves the AI at all.

  • 🔑

    Your key stays yours

    Stored only in your browser profile. AI providers are used with zero data retention.

FAQ

Questions

Does Navi cost anything?

No. Navi Free is free, with no account. Navi Pro uses your own OpenAI or Anthropic key, so the AI company bills you for what you use, usually a few cents an hour of active use. The panel shows today's cost.

Which browsers does it work in?

Google Chrome 139 or newer, on Windows, macOS and Linux. On-device speech recognition needs Chrome 139+. Other Chromium browsers may work but aren't tested.

Can I use it without seeing the screen or using a mouse?

Yes, that's who it's built for. After the one-time setup (allowing the mic), everything works by voice. Navi says where you are and what it did, reads pages and sections aloud, and lists headings, links and fields so you can jump straight to them. Push to talk (Ctrl + Space) works from the keyboard alone, and the panel's controls are labelled for screen readers.

Which speech recognizer should I use?

Parakeet (say “use Parakeet”) is the most accurate in everyday use; it downloads once (0.7 to 2.5 GB depending on your computer). Chrome's on-device recognizer is lighter and starts right away. Whisper understands about 100 languages but answers more slowly. All three run on your computer.

Do AirPods or a headset work?

Yes, though Bluetooth headsets switch to a lower-quality call mode while a mic is open, which makes music sound thinner and recognition a little harder. Navi points it out when it notices one. Your computer's own mic or a wired one gives the best results.

Will it act on things I say to other people?

Navi falls asleep after two quiet minutes, or when you say “go to sleep”, and ignores everything until “wake up”. While awake, a quick check tells requests apart from background talk and the TV.

How much memory does it use?

Models load only when they're needed, unload when idle, and are all freed when Navi is turned off. With Parakeet and Navi's voice loaded, expect roughly 1 to 3 GB.

Why isn't it on the Chrome Web Store?

Navi is early and changes quickly. It'll go on the store once it settles. Until then, loading it from a folder takes about two minutes (see Install above).

Is it open source?

Yes, under the MIT license. The code, the test suite, the speech benchmark and the research behind each choice are on GitHub.
